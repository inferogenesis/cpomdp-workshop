[session 1, nudge 1]
`observation_matrix` maps the position (x, y) to the reading (x, y).
The reading is the position itself, so the map is the identity matrix.
In jax, `jnp.eye(n)` builds the n-by-n identity.
Here n is 2, one for x and one for y.

[session 1, hint 1]
observation_matrix = jnp.eye(2)

[session 1, nudge 2]
`observation_noise` is R, how far a reading strays from the true position.
It is a covariance: each axis's variance on the diagonal, zeros elsewhere.
Here it is 0.05 on both axes.
A number times a matrix scales every entry, so `v * jnp.eye(n)` puts v on the diagonal.

[session 1, hint 2]
observation_noise = 0.05 * jnp.eye(2)

[session 2, nudge 1]
`pull` is the beacon's noise at x.
It is k times `distance_squared`, with the squared distance held to at most the cap.
`jnp.minimum(a, b)` returns the smaller of a and b, which caps a at b.
k is `params["beacon_gain"]` and the cap is `params["beacon_cap"]`.

[session 2, hint 1]
pull = params["beacon_gain"] * jnp.minimum(distance_squared, params["beacon_cap"])

[session 2, nudge 2]
k sets how fast the reading degrades away from the beacon.
Small k: the path stays nearly straight.
Large k: reading well near the beacon beats reaching the pad, and the rocket stops short.
Raise k from 0.08 in steps of about 0.1 and watch the top of the path.

[session 2, hint 2]
beacon_gain = 0.4

[session 3, nudge 1]
`blindness` is the blackout's noise at x.
It peaks at the blackout's centre and fades with distance, in a bell shape.
`jnp.exp(-d / w)` is 1 at d = 0 and falls toward 0 as d grows, more slowly for a larger w.
Here d is `from_blackout`, w is `width`, and `height` sets the peak.

[session 3, hint 1]
blindness = height * jnp.exp(-from_blackout / width)
